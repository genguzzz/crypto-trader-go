# Literature & open-source review: systematic alt-perp strategies (OKX USDT swaps)

Written 2026-10-10. Scope: ~200 most-traded alts (ex BTC/ETH), 1H/4H bars, long and short, max 5 positions, ~8x, fixed notional, ATR/swing stop capped at 200% of margin, one aggressiveness dial.
Every citation below was checked against a publisher, SSRN, arXiv, RePEc or repository page. Anything I could not verify was left out. "Verified" means title, authors and venue only. I read abstracts, not full papers.

In-house priors: Donchian ensembles on alts gave Sharpe ~0.16 (they only work on BTC/ETH). Shorting pumps has ~0 EV with fat squeeze tails. A ridge cross-sectional score had IC ~0.09 at 4h but did not hold up at our sizing. LightGBM short-only bottom-5 worked only in the 2025-26 alt bear.

---

## 1. Momentum and reversal in crypto

- **Liu & Tsyvinski (2021), "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency", *Review of Financial Studies* 34(6):2689-2727.** [SSRN 3226806](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3226806). Crypto returns don't load on stock or macro factors. There is a strong time-series momentum effect, and investor-attention proxies predict returns. *Use:* this supports trend signals on the market (BTC) as a regime input. The evidence is for the majors, not small alts.
- **Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu (2022), "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency", *Journal of Finance* 77(2):1133-1177**, doi:10.1111/jofi.13119. [SSRN 3379131](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3379131). Three factors (market, size, momentum) explain the cross-section. Ten characteristic-sorted long-short strategies are all absorbed by them. *Use:* alt returns are mostly CMKT beta plus size and momentum. A cross-sectional score that does not neutralise market beta is mostly a BTC-direction bet in disguise.
- **Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu (2025/26), "Cryptocurrency as an Investable Asset Class: Coming of Age"**, [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14435). A survey of seven stylised facts: a small factor set, frequent large jumps, and Sharpe comparable to other assets. *Use:* jumps are first-order, so stop gaps must be modelled.
- **Han, Kang & Ryu, "Time-Series and Cross-Sectional Momentum in the Cryptocurrency Market: A Comprehensive Analysis under Realistic Assumptions"** (working paper, [PDF](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)). Once trading costs and intraday price paths are included, many momentum portfolios get liquidated, and many "significant" ones earn insignificant profits. TSMOM evidence is strong and XSMOM evidence is weak. The gains are concentrated in large winners, while **losers often rebound and inflict large losses**. *Use:* this is the best match to our setting. It is direct evidence against systematic shorting of weak alts and against leveraged XS long-short. It also explains our pump-short result.
- **Fieberg, Liedtke, Metko & Zaremba (2023), "Cryptocurrency factor momentum", *Quantitative Finance* 23(12):1853-1869** ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v23y2023i12p1853-1869.html)). Covers 3,900+ coins and 34 anomalies over 2014-22. Factor momentum exists but comes mainly from size and volatility factors and ultimately from price momentum.
- **Grobys, Kolari, Sandretto, Shahzad & Äijö (2025), "Cryptocurrency momentum has (not) its moments", *Financial Markets and Portfolio Management*** (doi:10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9). Looks at the top 30 coins on weekly data, 2016-23. Momentum suffers severe crashes, and one coin can wipe out its significance. **Volatility management mitigates the crashes.** Momentum is a large-cap phenomenon.
- **Zaremba, Bilgin, Long, Mercik & Szczygielski (2021), "Up or down? Short-term reversal, momentum, and liquidity effects in cryptocurrency markets", *Int. Review of Financial Analysis* 78:101908** ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002349.html)). Across 3,600+ coins, the previous day's losers beat its winners. The reversal comes from illiquidity. **The largest, most liquid coins show daily momentum instead.** *Use:* the sign of a short-horizon signal should depend on liquidity rank. Expect continuation in the top ~30 and reversal in the illiquid tail of our 200.
- **Shen, Urquhart & Wang (2022), "Bitcoin intraday time-series momentum", *Financial Review*** ([Birmingham](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum/)). The first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour, and the effect is strongest in high-volume or high-volatility sessions. It is driven by liquidity provision.

**Takeaway:** time-series momentum survives in crypto, but mainly in large and liquid names. Leveraged cross-sectional momentum and short-the-loser books get killed by rebounds. Reversal lives in illiquid names.

## 2. Breakouts, channels and volatility management

- **Zarattini, Pagani & Barbon (2025), "Catching Crypto Trends; A Tactical Approach for Bitcoin and Altcoins"**, SFI Research Paper 25-80, [SSRN 5209907](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5209907).
  - Design: long-only Donchian ensemble with lookbacks of 5, 10, 20, 30, 60, 90, 150, 250 and 360 days. Entry is a close above the N-day max. The trailing stop is the midpoint of the N-day high/low and only ratchets up. Positions are resized daily to 25% annualised vol (90-day), capped at 2x.
  - Universe: survivorship-free, coins listed at least 1 year with 30-day median volume of at least $2M.
  - Result: on a top-20-liquidity rotational portfolio, Sharpe >1.5 and 10.8% annual alpha vs BTC.
  - *Use:* the edge comes from daily bars, long only, low leverage, vol-scaled sizing and the top-20 universe. Our Sharpe-0.16 result on 1H/4H bars across 200 alts at 8x is consistent with it, not a contradiction. Don't expect the edge to transfer down the liquidity curve or to the short side.
- **Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), "Time series momentum", *JFE*** ([SSRN 2089463](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)). TSMOM over 1-12 months across 58 futures. It performs best in extreme markets. This is the canonical vol-scaled trend construction.
- **Daniel & Moskowitz (2016), "Momentum crashes", *JFE*** ([NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)). Momentum crashes are partly forecastable. They happen in "panic" states after market declines with high volatility, when the market rebounds. A dynamic mean/variance-scaled version roughly doubles Sharpe. *Use:* short-momentum books are most dangerous right after an alt-market selloff.
- **Moreira & Muir (2017), "Volatility-Managed Portfolios", *Journal of Finance* 72(4):1611-1644** ([NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/22208)). Scaling exposure by inverse recent variance raises Sharpe because expected returns do not rise with volatility. Grobys et al. confirm this for crypto momentum.
- **Chandelier exit** (LeBeau; popularised in LeBeau & Lucas, *Computer Analysis of the Futures Markets*, 1992, and later Elder). The stop is the highest high minus k×ATR, with defaults 22 bars and k=3. This is a practitioner reference, not peer-reviewed.

I found no peer-reviewed source on Keltner or Donchian behaviour specifically in small caps. The evidence above (liquidity-dependent reversal, large-cap-only momentum) implies that breakouts in illiquid alts have a high false-break rate. Treat that as a hypothesis to test.

## 3. Regime, funding, open interest and liquidations

- **He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures"**, [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888). Derives no-arbitrage perp prices and bounds under trading costs. Perp-spot deviations are larger than in FX, **comove across coins**, and shrink over time. *Use:* the cross-coin average premium/funding is a market-wide sentiment and crowding gauge.
- **Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov, "Crypto carry"**, [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm) (2023, rev. 2025). Carry can reach about 60% p.a. It is driven by trend-chasing small investors plus scarce arbitrage capital. **High carry predicts future price crashes**, and rising carry goes with a rising price of crash insurance. *Use:* a high aggregate funding/basis is a de-risk signal for longs. Funding extremes support shorts only after price confirmation, because the crash timing is uncertain and squeezes are real.
- **Cheng, Deng, Wang & Yu (2021), "Liquidation, Leverage and Optimal Margin in Bitcoin Futures Markets"**, [arXiv 2102.04591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04591). Uses BitMEX perps. Daily liquidations average 3.51% (longs) and 1.89% (shorts) of open interest, and liquidated traders average about 60x leverage. With EVT tails, a 1% daily margin-call probability requires about **3x leverage for longs and 5x for shorts on BTC**. *Use:* at 8x on alts (several times BTC's volatility), daily liquidation probability is far from negligible. Stops must sit well inside the liquidation price, and isolated vs cross margin matters (see §4).
- **"Autodeleveraging: Impossibilities and Optimization"**, [arXiv 2512.01112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01112). Documents the 10 Oct 2025 cascade, including about $2.1B of positions ADL'd on Hyperliquid in 12 minutes. *Use:* in a cascade, stop orders slip and profitable shorts can be force-closed. Backtests must include a gap/ADL stress scenario.
- **Luo (2025), "Three essays on futures expected returns..."**, PhD thesis, [Edinburgh](https://era.ed.ac.uk/handle/1842/43608). A cost-of-carry model for crypto perps in which a two-factor model (log basis plus price-volume) explains 63 significant predictors. It also finds strong **category (sector) momentum**. *Use:* sector-relative momentum (L1s, memes, AI and so on) is a cheap feature to test.
- **Regime and breadth.** Daniel-Moskowitz and Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu together justify conditioning on the market factor. In practice that means BTC trend plus alt breadth (% of alts above their N-bar MA) and cross-sectional dispersion. I found no verified paper dedicated to an altcoin-breadth filter, so this is an in-house test item.

## 4. Stops, sizing, leverage

- **Kaminski & Lo (2014), "When do stop-loss rules stop losses?", *J. Financial Markets* 18:234-254** ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)). Under a random walk, 0/1 stops always lower expected return. **They add value only when returns have momentum or serial dependence**, and at longer sampling frequencies they can raise return while cutting volatility. *Use:* stops are part of the alpha, not just risk control. They help in trend strategies but hurt mean-reversion (fade) strategies, which should rely on time stops and small size instead.
- **MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba, "Good and bad properties of the Kelly criterion"** (2010; in *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion*, World Scientific 2011; [PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)). Full Kelly maximises long-run growth but bets are very large and risky in the short run. **Fractional Kelly** keeps most of the growth with far less drawdown.
- **Vince (1992), *The Mathematics of Money Management*, Wiley** (ISBN 978-0-471-54738-9). Optimal f, fixed-fractional trading, and the asymmetric damage from over-betting beyond optimal f.
- **Leverage and liquidation mechanics (OKX).** OKX tiered maintenance-margin ratios start around 0.5-1% for liquid perps and rise with position size ([OKX help](https://www.okx.com/help-center/6974553900045)).
  - In **isolated** mode at 8x, the position is liquidated at roughly a 1/8 − MMR ≈ 11-12% adverse move. A loss of 100% of margin is the ceiling, so a "200% of margin / 25% move" cap can never be reached.
  - The 25% cap only makes sense in **cross** mode, where one trade can draw down 2× its nominal margin from shared equity.
  - **Design consequence:** treat the cap as a price-distance cap of at most about 10% (isolated), or keep cross mode but size so that the worst gap across 5 positions stays within the equity budget.
- **Freqtrade docs** ([stoploss](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/stoploss/), [callbacks](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-callbacks/)). On futures, the stoploss is **risk on stake**, not price distance: a 10% stop at 10x fires at a 1% move. `custom_stoploss` with `stoploss_from_absolute` implements ATR stops (example: 2×ATR). `custom_exit` implements time stops. Use `stoploss_on_exchange` (supported on OKX) to avoid latency.

## 5. ML for return prediction and validation

- **Cakici, Shahzad, Będowska-Sójka & Zaremba (2024), "Machine learning and the cross-section of cryptocurrency returns", *IRFA* 94:103244** ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001765.html)). All ML methods earn substantial gains, but **added complexity adds little**. Predictability comes from a handful of features: price level, past alpha, illiquidity and momentum. **Alpha comes from the long leg** (unlike equities) and is concentrated in hard-to-trade coins. *Use:* this matches our ridge ≈ LightGBM experience. Prefer a small linear or monotone model and keep the long leg. Haircut alpha heavily in the illiquid tail.
- **López de Prado (2018), *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, Wiley.** Triple-barrier labels (TP, SL, time limit) and **meta-labeling** (a secondary model decides whether to take or size a primary signal) are in Ch. 3. Purged K-fold with embargo is in Ch. 7. *Use:* label with the exact barriers we trade (ATR stop, R-multiple target, time stop). Use ML only as a meta-filter on a rule-based primary signal, not as the signal itself.
- **Bailey & López de Prado (2014), "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio", *JPM* 40(5):94-107**, [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551). Corrects Sharpe for the number of trials and for non-normality.
- **Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2015/17), "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting", *J. Computational Finance***, [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253). Estimates PBO with combinatorially symmetric CV (CSCV). *Use:* log every config tried, and report DSR and PBO for the final pick. Our earlier studies ran dozens of variants, so a Sharpe of 0.5 on alts is likely noise.

## 6. Open-source references

- **freqtrade-strategies** ([GitHub](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade-strategies), GPL-3). Educational strategies built from `minimal_roi` tables, a static stoploss and indicator entries. Most are spot and long-only and hyperopt-fitted, with an explicit warning that the backtests are illustrative. Useful for plumbing, not for edge.
- **NostalgiaForInfinity** ([GitHub](https://github.com/iterativv/NostalgiaForInfinity)). The most popular community freqtrade strategy, built on dip-buying with many entry conditions. The authors recommend 4-6 open trades on a 40-80-pair volume pairlist. Patterns to borrow: a volume-ranked dynamic pairlist and a small concurrent-trade cap. Its many conditions are a textbook overfitting case.
- **Jesse** ([GitHub](https://github.com/jesse-ai/jesse), MIT). Futures, leverage, multi-symbol and multi-timeframe without look-ahead, Optuna optimisation, **Monte Carlo** and **rule-significance testing against random entries**. Worth copying: the random-entry benchmark for every rule.
- **Hummingbot V2** ([controllers](https://condor.hummingbot.org/bots/controllers), [MACD-BB blog](https://hummingbot.org/blog/directional-trading-with-macd-and-bollinger-bands/)). Directional controllers (Bollinger V1, MACD-BB, SuperTrend) launch PositionExecutors on perps with a **triple barrier**: stop loss, take profit, time limit and trailing stop. This is the cleanest public pattern for exactly our trade lifecycle.

Common pattern across these repos: volume-filtered pairlist, a cap on concurrent trades, fixed stake, a hard SL plus trailing activation after profit, and a time stop. Almost none use vol-scaled sizing or regime filters, which is where the academic evidence says the edge is.

## Design implications

### (a) Conservative strategy: "liquid trend-long with regime gate"
1. **Universe = top ~30-40 by 30-day OKX perp volume, not 200.** Momentum is large-cap only (Grobys; Zaremba; Cakici's alpha sits in hard-to-trade coins). Test: Sharpe by liquidity decile.
2. **Long-biased Donchian/close-breakout ensemble on 4H plus 1D confirmation**, with lookbacks spanning about 5-90 days (Zarattini et al.). Shorts only with a regime flag (6). Test: long-only vs symmetric.
3. **Regime gate:** trade longs only when BTC is above its 50/200-day trend and alt breadth (% above the 20-day MA) exceeds a threshold. Cut size after sharp market drops with high volatility (Daniel-Moskowitz panic state; Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu market factor).
4. **Funding/basis de-risk:** stop opening new longs when the cross-coin mean funding is in its top decile (Schmeling et al.; He et al.).
5. **Inverse-volatility notional.** Scale notional so each trade risks the same fraction of equity at the ATR stop, with leverage set to fit. Do not use a fixed 8x (Moreira-Muir; Grobys). Test: fixed vs vol-scaled notional on identical signals.
6. **Stops:** initial stop at 2-3×ATR(4H) or below the last swing low, whichever is tighter, and always at least 3 percentage points inside isolated liquidation. Then a chandelier trail at 3×ATR or the Donchian-midpoint trail (Kaminski-Lo: stops pay off with momentum).
7. **Risk per trade ≤ 0.5-1% of equity, i.e. ¼ Kelly or less.** Five positions in correlated alts behave like 2-3 bets (MacLean-Thorp-Ziemba).

### (b) Aggressive strategy: "squeeze-aware breakout + meta-labelled filter, both sides"
8. **Primary signal:** 1H/4H volatility-contraction breakout (ATR or Bollinger-width percentile low, then a close beyond the N-bar range on above-median volume). Signal direction follows the liquidity rule: continuation in the top 50 and no fading of illiquid names (Zaremba).
9. **Meta-label with a small model** (logistic/ridge/shallow GBM on about 10 features: momentum, sector momentum, funding z-score, OI change, BTC beta, illiquidity). Labels are triple-barrier with our exact stop, R-target and 24-48h time stop, under purged/embargoed CV (López de Prado; Cakici: simple beats complex; Luo: category momentum).
10. **Shorts only on confirmed breakdowns in liquid names, never on pump tops.** Require price to close below the swing low, funding above zero (crowded longs), and a gate that blocks entries right after a market capitulation. Reason: losers rebound (Han-Kang-Ryu) and momentum crashes come in rebounds (Daniel-Moskowitz).
11. **Hard time stop.** Exit at 2-3× the median holding time of winners if the trade hasn't reached +1R (Hummingbot/AFML triple barrier). Test the time-stop sweep separately.
12. **Aggressiveness dial = fractional Kelly multiplier** on risk per trade (e.g. 0.25 → 0.75 of estimated Kelly), with notional derived from the stop distance and a cap on aggregate gross exposure (≤ 3-4× equity). Validate the dial with Monte Carlo trade-resampling (Jesse) and report risk of ruin. Report DSR and PBO for each strategy (Bailey et al.), and run a 10-Oct-2025-style 15-20% gap/ADL stress (arXiv 2512.01112).

**Gating check for both strategies:** reconcile the "200% of margin" cap with the margin mode before any backtest. At 8x isolated the stop must sit within about 10% of entry, because otherwise liquidation (≈11-12%) fires first.
